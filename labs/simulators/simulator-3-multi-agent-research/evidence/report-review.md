# Impact of AI on creative industries

## Summary

The evidence is mostly about adoption. It holds one reported income effect (freelance writers) and one time saving (previsualisation). It does not support sector-wide, cross-sector or impact conclusions. Six claims were gathered. Only one is verified. Four are single-source and not checked, and awaiting verification. One returned a different record in its check and stays unconfirmed. Visual arts has no evidence at all because the searches failed. That is a gap in the record, not a finding.

Date format used throughout: "published YYYY-MM-DD; figure period X or not stated".

## Established findings

Only one finding is verified.

- **Creative SME adoption of generative AI (C5).** WIPO reports generative AI adoption among creative SMEs at 41% in 2025, up from 23% in 2023 (WIPO-Creative-Economy-2025.pdf, p.14 [1]; published 2025-11-30; figure period 2025, baseline 2023). Verification: verified. The unit is creative SMEs. The figure is not sector-specific. WIPO's weighting is not stated. Only one page excerpt was loaded. This is the only two-point comparison in the evidence, and it applies to creative SMEs in this one source.

The sector claims below are reported as attributed to their sources. None is established.

## Contested findings

| Definition | Generative AI adoption | "Adoption"; AI type not stated, not defined as generative AI |
| Weighting | Not stated | Methodology weighted by firm size; described as an estimate |
| Prior comparison | 23% in 2023 | None given |
| Verification | Verified | Check returned a different record, unconfirmed |

The gap is 14 percentage points. Both values are kept as reported. This report does not average them, prefer one, or call the gap a trend. The definitions differ (generative AI versus unspecified adoption), and the weighting differs (stated for UNESCO, not stated for WIPO). The figures are not like-for-like. The sources do not establish why they differ.

On verification, the tool returned the WIPO 41% record when asked about UNESCO's 55%. That leaves C6 neither confirmed nor contradicted. It rests on the excerpt alone.

## Findings by sector

Each figure stays in its own section with its unit named. Do not rank or combine them. The populations and measures differ.

### Music

- **Labels (C1).** 62% of surveyed labels reported using generative tools in production during 2025 (IFPI Global Music Report 2026 [3]; published 2026-03-19; figure period 2025). Unit: labels. Sample size, regions and the definition of "in production" are not stated. Verification: not checked, single source, awaiting verification.
- **Union members (C2).** 48% of Musicians' Union members reported using AI tools in composition or mixing (Musicians' Union member survey [4]; published 2025-09-02; figure period not stated). Unit: union members, self-reported. Sample size is not stated. It covers adoption only, not income, jobs or artist responses. Verification: not checked, single source, awaiting verification.

The two figures measure different units, so they do not compare. The corpus appears to hold only two music documents.

### Writing and publishing

- **Freelance writers (C3).** In an Authors Guild AI survey, freelance writers reported a median income effect of AI of -11% year over year (Authors Guild AI survey [5]; published 2026-01-14; figure period not stated). Unit: freelance writers, self-reported. Survey period, sample size and methodology are not stated. Do not generalise to authors, journalists, copywriters or translators. Verification: not checked, single source, awaiting verification.

### Film and TV

- **Productions (C4).** A Visual Effects Society workforce note reports that previsualisation time fell by roughly 30% on productions using AI storyboarding (Visual Effects Society workforce note [6]; published 2026-02-07; figure period not stated). Unit: productions using AI storyboarding. No baseline, measurement period or cause is stated. It says nothing about jobs, budgets, post-production, or film and TV as a whole. Verification: not checked, single source, awaiting verification.

## Coverage gaps

- **Visual arts:** no data on illustrators, photographers, designers or fine artists (income, commissions), image-generator adoption, art market activity, or copyright lawsuits and opt-out tools such as Glaze and Nightshade.
- **Music:** nothing on revenues, jobs or displacement, royalties, streaming-platform policy (AI-track labelling or removals), label-to-AI-generator licensing deals, copyright disputes or lawsuits, or artist responses such as campaigns or opt-outs. The IFPI sample size and regions are not in the excerpt.
- **Writing and publishing:** nothing on AI-generated books or marketplace flooding, training-data licensing deals, lawsuits or settlements, journalists, copywriters or translators, or publishers' own adoption or revenue. The Authors Guild sample size, methodology and population are not given. Suggested queries: "Anthropic authors copyright settlement", "Amazon KDP AI-generated books limits", "News publishers AI licensing deals", and a stand-alone "translators AI income survey". A different search tool or corpus could cover litigation and licensing.
- **Film and TV:** nothing on generative video tools (for example Sora, Veo, Runway), AI dubbing or post-production beyond previsualisation, jobs or budget effects, or WGA and SAG-AFTRA agreements, strikes, studio deals or lawsuits.
- **Cross-sector:** no sector-specific figures were extracted from either document. No economy-wide figures (value, employment, revenue) were returned. Figure dates are year-only. UNESCO's definition of "adoption" and its prior-year comparison are not stated. Only one page excerpt per document was loaded, so other pages may hold relevant statistics.
- **Corroboration and verification:** C1 to C4 each rest on one source. Only C5 was confirmed. The C6 check returned a different record.

## Review

### Cross-section conflicts

- **C5 and C6:** same year and population label, 14 points apart, with different definitions and weighting. Shown side by side. The cause of the gap is not established. C5 is verified. C6 is unconfirmed, not contradicted.
- **C1, C2, C5, C6:** these measure different units (labels, union members, creative SMEs). Placing them together would invite false comparisons or a pseudo-trend. They stay in separate sections.
- **Date labelling (C1 to C6):** the bundles mixed publication dates and figure periods. This report uses one format: "published YYYY-MM-DD; figure period X or not stated". For C2, C3 and C4 the figure period is not stated.
- **Verification labels (C1 to C6):** three fixed labels apply. "Verified" for C5. "Not checked, single source, awaiting verification" for C1 to C4. "Check returned a different record, unconfirmed" for C6.
- **Verdict inconsistency (C2, C3, C4):** reviewers rated C4 supported, though its excerpt has the same gaps as C2 and C3, which were rated overstated. The excerpt for C4 appears to restate the claim. C4 is supported only for the narrow statement that the source reports roughly 30%. It carries no causal wording and is no stronger than the other single-source figures.

### Coverage gaps by severity

- **High:** visual arts (no claims; two timeouts, one empty result, budget used up).
- **High:** impact evidence in every sector. All claims measure adoption (C1, C2, C5, C6), one reported income effect (C3) or one time saving (C4). Nothing covers revenue, jobs, royalties, copyright and licensing, lawsuits, strikes, guild agreements or artist responses.
- **High:** writing and publishing. One self-reported freelance-writer figure with unknown period, sample and method.
- **Medium:** music (two adoption figures with different units).
- **Medium:** film and TV (one figure with no baseline).
- **Medium:** other creative sectors and topics. No evidence on games, advertising and design, photography, architecture, performing arts, regions, or small versus large firms.
- **Medium:** corroboration. C1 to C4 have no second source, and the C6 verification mismatch has not been rerun.

### Rewordings and why

- **C1:** reworded to state the survey basis, 2025 period, and missing sample size, regions and definition.
- **C2:** reworded to keep the self-reported basis and note the missing reference period and sample size.
- **C3:** reworded to report a "median income effect of -11%" as reported by respondents. It does not say income fell, because the survey period, sample and method are unknown.
- **C4:** reworded to attribute the figure to the source and remove causal language.
- **C6:** reworded to say "adoption" is not defined as generative AI and the figure is unconfirmed, not contradicted.
- **C5:** stays as written.

### Overall assessment

The evidence is thin and uneven. It has six claims, mostly single-source and unchecked, and mostly about adoption rather than impact. Each figure can be stated as attributed to its source. No sector-wide, cross-sector or trend conclusion holds. Only C5 has a two-point comparison, limited to creative SMEs in that source. Visual arts is an explicit gap from search failures. Retry that search, or use the WIPO PDF, before publication if coverage of the sector is wanted.
