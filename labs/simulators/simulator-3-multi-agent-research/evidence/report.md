# Impact of AI on creative industries

Report date: 2026-11-05. Evidence is thin and mostly single-source. Each claim carries its source and publication date. Sector coverage is uneven, and visual arts has no findings at all.

## Established findings

"Established" here means no conflicting figure was found. It does not mean corroborated. Every item below rests on a single excerpt, and none has a second source.

- **Creative SME baseline, 2023.** WIPO reports 23% generative AI adoption among creative SMEs in 2023 (WIPO-Creative-Economy-2025.pdf, p. 14, published 2025-11-30). UNESCO gives no 2023 figure, so nothing contradicts it. The excerpt states no methodology. This is the baseline for the contested 41% figure, so it carries the same unknown definition and sample.
- **Music, labels.** 62% of surveyed labels used generative tools in production during 2025 (IFPI Global Music Report 2026, published 2026-03-19). This is a share of surveyed labels, not a market-wide share.
- **Music, musicians.** 48% of responding Musicians' Union members reported using AI tools in composition or mixing (Musicians' Union member survey, published 2025-09-02). This is a share of members who responded, so response bias is possible. It covers AI tools, not strictly generative tools.
- **Writing and publishing.** The median reported income effect of AI on freelance writers is -11% year over year (Authors Guild AI survey, published 2026-01-14). The evidence is thin. The excerpt is one line with no sample size, methodology or definition of "freelance writers". The same single result came back for all queries.
- **Film and television.** Productions using AI storyboarding report roughly a 30% reduction in previsualisation time (Visual Effects Society workforce note, published 2026-02-07). This is a pre-production point only. It says nothing about jobs, costs or other stages.

**Populations are not comparable.** The IFPI figure (surveyed labels), the Musicians' Union figure (responding union members) and the WIPO and UNESCO figures (creative SMEs) cover different populations. Do not compare or combine them.

## Contested findings

Two sources give different 2025 adoption figures for creative SMEs. They are shown side by side and are not reconciled.

| | WIPO | UNESCO |
|---|---|---|
| Value | 41% in 2025 (up from 23% in 2023) | Estimated at 55% in 2025 |
| Source | WIPO-Creative-Economy-2025.pdf, p. 14 | UNESCO-Culture-Report-2026.pdf, p. 88 |
| Published | 2025-11-30 | 2026-04-01 |
| Population | Creative SMEs in general, no sector named | Creative SMEs, no sector named |
| Caveat | The excerpt gives no methodology. It does not say how adoption is defined, what the sample is, or whether results are weighted. | The figure is an estimate, and the methodology weights by firm size. The excerpt does not say explicitly that the technology measured is generative AI, so its definition of "adoption" may differ from WIPO's. |

- **Possible cause, unconfirmed.** The document-analysis agent suggests the two sources may differ in definition and weighting. Neither excerpt confirms this.
- **Verification.**
  - The WIPO claim (41%, p. 14, 2025-11-30) was verified (verified=true).
  - The UNESCO check (55%, p. 88, 2026-04-01) returned verified=false. The fact bank answered with the WIPO record (41%) rather than a UNESCO record. The result is **inconclusive, not a refutation**, and the 55% figure remains unconfirmed against the fact bank.
