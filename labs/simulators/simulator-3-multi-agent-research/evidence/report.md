# Impact of AI on Creative Industries: Evidence Report

## Headline finding

The evidence base is thin and partly unverified. Most claims rest on single search excerpts from example.org placeholder domains, and no claim has a verified original source. Visual arts and design returned no findings at all. The one statistic with two published sources, creative-SME generative AI adoption for 2025, conflicts sharply between WIPO and UNESCO, and this report does not reconcile them.

## Established findings

These are the claims the synthesis labels "established." Each rests on a single unverified excerpt, so the label is the synthesis's, not independent confirmation.

- **Music, label-side tool use:** 62% of surveyed labels used generative tools in music production during 2025. Source: IFPI Global Music Report 2026, published 2026-03-19. Only a single excerpt was seen, with no methodology, sample, or corroboration. Label-side survey only.
- **Music, union members:** 48% of Musicians' Union members reported using AI tools in composition or mixing. Source: Musicians' Union member survey, published 2025-09-02. The sample is not representative of all musicians.
- **Freelance writing income:** The median reported income effect of AI on freelance writers was −11% year over year. Source: Authors Guild AI survey, published 2026-01-14. The original was not located.
- **Film previsualisation:** On productions using AI storyboarding, previsualisation time fell by roughly 30%. Source: Visual Effects Society workforce note, published 2026-02-07. No sample size, production count, or measurement basis was available.

## Contested findings

| Claim | WIPO | UNESCO |
|---|---|---|
| Generative AI adoption among creative SMEs, 2025 | 41% (WIPO-Creative-Economy-2025.pdf, p. 14, published 2025-11-30) | 55%, estimate (UNESCO-Culture-Report-2026.pdf, p. 88, published 2026-04-01) |

- The gap is 14 percentage points for the same year and population. Possible causes (definition of "adoption," actual versus estimated figures, weighting) are not established.
- WIPO's excerpt states no method or sample definition.
- UNESCO's figure is an estimate weighted by firm size, and it does not define "adoption."
- The fact bank returned the WIPO 41% record for the UNESCO 55% query, so the UNESCO figure remains unverified.
- WIPO also reports a 2023 baseline of 23%, which has no independent source.

## Coverage gaps

- **Visual arts and design: no findings.** All three searches timed out, and they are retryable. This is a research gap, not evidence of no impact.
- **Music:** Nothing on distribution, licensing, rights and royalties, revenue, streaming, AI track volumes, or artist income.
- **Writing and publishing:** Nothing on publisher or editor practice, contracts, AI clauses, translation, audiobooks, copyright disputes, journalism, or disclosure rules.
- **Film and television:** No independent sources on scriptwriting, dubbing and localization, union bargaining, jobs, audience effects, or copyright.
- **WIPO and UNESCO:** Only one excerpt each was extracted, so other pages are unread.

## Recommended follow-up

1. **Visual arts and design:** Retry the timed-out searches. This is the largest gap.
2. **Resolve WIPO vs. UNESCO:** Retrieve both methodology sections (sample definitions, the meaning of "adoption," weighting) and decide whether the figures are comparable.
3. **Verify originals:** Locate the primary IFPI, Musicians' Union, Authors Guild, and Visual Effects Society documents, and replace the example.org excerpts.
4. **Music economics:** Search distribution, licensing, royalties, streaming, and AI track volume.
5. **Writing and publishing:** Search contracts, AI clauses, translation, audiobooks, copyright disputes, and disclosure rules.
6. **Film and television:** Search scriptwriting, dubbing, union bargaining, and copyright with independent sources.
7. **Fact-bank integrity:** Investigate why the UNESCO query returned the WIPO record, and check other queries for similar mismatches.
