# AI and the creative industries: a cited report

Report date: 2026-11-05. Coverage is uneven. Music, writing and publishing, and film and TV each rest on one or two sources. Visual arts has no findings. The two document findings conflict and are shown side by side.

## Established findings

Each finding below comes from a single web source. None supplies an evidence type, method or sample size, so treat each as one source's reported figure. "Established" is the synthesis status, not a sign of strong corroboration. Each figure describes its own stated population only. None describes the whole creative economy, and none claims a cause.

**Music**
- IFPI Global Music Report 2026 (published 2026-03-19) reports that 62% of surveyed labels used generative AI tools in production during 2025. The figure covers surveyed labels only.
- The Musicians' Union member survey (published 2025-09-02) reports that 48% of members used AI tools in composition or mixing. The figure covers union members who responded, not all musicians.
- Both music findings cover adoption only. Both stem from one source each.

**Writing and publishing**
- The Authors Guild AI survey (published 2026-01-14) reports a median income effect of -11% year over year for freelance writers. The population is freelance writers. The finding reports a figure and does not establish a cause.

**Film and television**
- The Visual Effects Society workforce note (published 2026-02-07) reports a roughly 30% reduction in previsualisation time on productions that used AI storyboarding. The figure is approximate and states no sample.

**Visual arts**
- No findings. See Coverage gaps.

## Coverage gaps

- **Music (partial coverage, not an error).** All three queries returned the same two adoption sources, IFPI and the Musicians' Union. Nothing was found on musicians' revenue or employment, royalties and streaming, licensing deals, or copyright disputes.
- **Writing and publishing (failure type: thin_results).** All three queries returned only the Authors Guild survey on freelance writer income. There is no evidence for journalists, copywriters or translators, publisher adoption, AI-generated books, training-data licensing or copyright litigation. Suggested alternatives: use more specific queries (named lawsuits or publisher names), or add sources outside this corpus. Attempted queries: "AI in book publishing: publisher adoption and AI-generated books 2026; AI training data licensing and copyright litigation authors publishers 2026".
- **Film and television (failure type: thin_results).** All three queries returned the same single hit, the VES workforce note. No structured error was returned, so no retry was triggered, and the query budget of three is used up. Nothing was found on WGA or SAG-AFTRA agreements or strikes, studio licensing deals, legal disputes, dubbing, script development, generative video, or job and budget effects. Suggested alternatives: run narrower queries in a separate pass (for example "SAG-AFTRA AI contract 2026", "Disney Universal lawsuit Midjourney", "AI dubbing film jobs survey"), or report these as gaps.
- **Visual arts (coverage none; failure type: timeout, retryable).** All three queries timed out, with attempts recorded as 1, 2 and 3 and empty partial results. No further retry was made. The queries were:
  1. "AI image generators impact on illustrators, designers, photographers income and employment visual arts 2026"

  3. "AI training data copyright rulings visual artists lawsuits Stability AI Midjourney Getty 2026"

  There are no statistics or citations for this sector. The two PDF findings have no sector breakdown, so they do not fill the gap. Suggested alternatives: retry once with a narrower query, ask document-analysis to cover visual arts from the WIPO PDF, or report visual arts as a gap. This needs a new search or document pass by the coordinator.
- **Internal PDFs.** Neither adoption figure has a sector breakdown, a stated sample or a stated method. Both stay classed "unclear" and have not been upgraded. The data type behind the UNESCO estimate (survey, market data or model) is unknown. Resolving the discrepancy would need the full report text or methodology annexes, which this pass did not cover.
- **Web findings.** No evidence type, methodology or sample size was supplied for the IFPI, Musicians' Union, Authors Guild or VES findings, so their scope and method are unverified here. The IFPI figure covers "surveyed labels" only, and the VES figure is "roughly" 30% with no stated sample.
