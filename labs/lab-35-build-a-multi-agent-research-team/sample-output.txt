# Impact of AI on Creative Industries: Evidence Synthesis from Surveys, Trackers and Internal Documents

Available evidence is thin and mostly survey-based, with unverified methods and some vendor or advocacy sources. Reported adoption is high in music, design and small creative firms, and income and commission losses appear in writing and illustration. Film and television evidence is minimal, and no sector is supported by peer-reviewed sector studies. The two cross-sector adoption figures measure different things and should not be compared.

- **Music**: 62% of surveyed independent labels used generative AI tools in music production during 2025. Source type not stated, apparently a label-focused publisher survey; methodology unverified. (Northbridge Label Survey 2026, https://example.org/northbridge-label-survey-2026, published 2026-03-19)
- **Music**: 48% of Session Players Guild members used AI tools for composition or mixing at least once a month. This is a membership poll and may not represent session musicians overall. (Session Players Guild member poll, https://example.org/session-players-guild-poll, published 2025-09-02)
- **Writing**: Median freelance copywriting income fell 11% year over year, and 6 in 10 respondents blamed AI drafting tools. Industry survey by a freelance writers' organization; based on an excerpt only, full study not retrieved. (Freelance Writers Collective income study, https://example.org/freelance-writers-collective-income, published 2026-01-14)
- **Writing**: 19 of 25 surveyed book publishers now require authors to disclose AI assistance. This is a publisher-run tracker; how the 25 publishers were selected is not stated. (Lanternhouse Publishers AI policy tracker, https://example.org/lanternhouse-ai-policy-tracker, published 2026-04-02)
- **Film and Television**: 44% of independent film productions used AI for visual-effects cleanup in 2025. This is a partial result from a timed-out search; source type and methodology are unverified, and the sector is very thin and not representative. (Indie Screen Council VFX survey, https://example.org/indie-screen-council-vfx, published 2026-06-08)
- **Visual Arts and Design**: 37% of member illustrators surveyed said they lost at least one commission to AI image tools in 2025. This is a self-reported advocacy/membership poll and is not representative. (Illustrators Circle flash poll, https://example.org/illustrators-circle-flash-poll, published 2026-05-12)
- **Visual Arts and Design**: 71% of graphic design studios surveyed use AI image generation for concept work. This is an industry survey; methodology and sample were not reported in the finding. (Design Studio Pulse 2026, https://example.org/design-studio-pulse-2026, published 2026-02-20)
- **Cross-sector adoption (internal documents)**: Generative AI adoption among creative small firms reached 41% in 2025, up from 23% in 2023. This is the only peer-reviewed source, based on a stratified random sample of 3,100 firms in 12 countries; only p.112 and a short summary were readable, 'creative small firms' is undefined, the 2023 figure is unsourced, and the 12 countries are unnamed. No sector-specific statements are visible. (Halden-Rao-2026-Journal-of-Creative-Economies.pdf, p.112; peer-reviewed journal article published 2026-03-15 (full title, authors and publisher not returned))
- **Cross-sector adoption (internal documents)**: 78% of creative studios use generative AI every week. This is a non-peer-reviewed vendor benchmark by a company selling AI image tools, based on an opt-in poll of 640 customers; it is unclear whether 78% refers to all 640 or a subset, and 'creative studios' is undefined. Only p.3 and a summary were readable, and no sector-specific statements are visible. It measures a different thing from the Halden-Rao figure and should not be compared directly. (BrightCanvas-Customer-Benchmark-2026.pdf, p.3; vendor benchmark published 2026-06-01)
